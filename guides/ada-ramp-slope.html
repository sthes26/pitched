ADA ramp slope: the 1:12 rule

Short answer: an ADA ramp can rise at most 1 inch for every 12 inches of run (8.33%). Each run can rise at most 30 inches, and every run needs a level landing at least 60 inches long at the top and bottom.

Add the landings to the length: at least 60 inches at the top, the bottom and between runs.

The other ramp rules (2010 ADA Standards, section 405)

ItemRule
Running slope1:12 max (405.2)
Cross slope1:48 max, about 2% (405.3)
Clear width36 in min between handrails (405.5)
Rise per run30 in max (405.6)
LandingsAt least as wide as the ramp and 60 in long; 60 by 60 in where the ramp turns (405.7)
HandrailsBoth sides when the rise is more than 6 in, 34 to 38 in high (405.8, 505)
Edge protectionA curb or barrier so a wheel can't slip off (405.9)

A ramp at a house is often built to the IRC instead, which also caps slope at 1:12 (R311.8). Public and commercial ramps follow the ADA Standards and the IBC (1012).

These are the model code numbers. Your state or city may have adopted a different edition or made amendments. Always confirm requirements with your building official.

How to check an existing ramp

  1. Measure the slope along the ramp in a few places. Any spot steeper than 8.33% fails.
  2. Measure across the ramp. More than 2% fails.
  3. Measure each landing's length and check that handrails run the full length.
