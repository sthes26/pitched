Stair riser and tread code

Short answer: in a house (IRC), risers can be up to 7 3/4 inches and treads must be at least 10 inches. In other buildings (IBC), risers are 4 to 7 inches and treads at least 11 inches. In both, risers and treads in one flight can't differ by more than 3/8 inch.

These are the model code numbers. Your state or city may have adopted a different edition or made amendments. Always confirm requirements with your building official.

How to lay out a stair

  1. Measure the total rise from finished floor to finished floor, not subfloor to subfloor.
  2. Divide by the largest riser allowed (7.75 for a house) and round up. That's the number of risers.
  3. Divide the total rise by that number to get the actual riser height.
  4. Treads are one fewer than risers. Multiply by the tread depth for the total run.

Example: 105 inches of total rise ÷ 7.75 = 13.5, so 14 risers of 7 1/2 inches, 13 treads, and 130 inches of run with 10 inch treads.

Where stairs fail inspection

  • The first or last riser is off because the finished floor thickness was forgotten. This breaks the 3/8 inch rule.
  • Handrail missing or too low on short runs of 4 risers.
  • Headroom under a beam or soffit at the top of a basement stair.
  • Open risers that let a 4 inch sphere through (IRC R311.7.5.1).
